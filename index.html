My name is Angel Pivaral , and I am a senior Biochemistry major at University of California, Riverside, set to graduate in 2026. Throughout my undergraduate career, I have developed a deep appreciation for the molecular mechanisms that drive life—from metabolic pathways to protein structure—and I am eager to apply this knowledge in a research or industry setting after graduation. While my primary focus is biochemistry, I have also cultivated a strong interest in the technical and computational side of science. This class and my GitHub portfolio represent an opportunity to showcase not only my scientific training but also my growing proficiency in coding, computer troubleshooting, and hardware assembly—skills that are increasingly valuable in modern scientific research. My journey into technology began as a personal hobby. I have always been the kind of person who wants to understand how things work, whether that’s a biochemical pathway or a motherboard. Over the years, I have built several computers from scratch, selecting compatible components, assembling the hardware, installing operating systems, and troubleshooting driver conflicts. This hands-on experience has taught me patience, attention to detail, and a systematic approach to problem-solving—qualities that translate directly to laboratory work and computational analysis. I am comfortable diagnosing hardware failures, upgrading components, and optimizing system performance, and I often serve as the informal "tech support" for friends and family.

In parallel, I have gained a foundation in basic coding, primarily using Python and R for data analysis and visualization. I have written scripts to process experimental data, automate repetitive calculations, and generate graphs for lab reports. While I do not consider myself an expert programmer, I am comfortable with variables, loops, functions, and basic data structures, and I am continually expanding my skills. I have also used Git and GitHub to version-control my code, collaborate on group projects, and document my work. I understand the importance of clear commit messages, branching, and README files, and I appreciate how these practices make scientific code more reproducible and transparent. For this class, I am excited to use GitHub not just as a storage space but as a professional portfolio that demonstrates my ability to learn new tools and communicate technical work effectively. What sets me apart is my ability to bridge the gap between the wet lab and the computer. Biochemistry is increasingly data-driven, and I believe that a scientist who can troubleshoot a PCR machine, write a Python script to analyze spectrophotometry data, and build a reliable workstation is a more versatile and effective researcher. I am a proactive learner who enjoys tackling unfamiliar challenges, whether that means optimizing a protein purification protocol or debugging a segmentation fault in my code. I am also a strong communicator and team player—I have presented research posters, worked in collaborative lab groups, and tutored peers in both biochemistry and basic computer skills. As I approach graduation, I am excited about the possibilities that lie ahead. I am particularly interested in bioinformatics, computational biology, and lab automation, where my biochemistry background and technical skills can combine to solve complex biological problems. I am also open to roles in research and development, quality control, or technical support within the life sciences industry. In the short term, I aim to strengthen my coding abilities through this class and by contributing to small open-source projects or personal data-analysis repositories. I believe that continuous learning is essential, and I treat every project—whether in the lab or on GitHub—as an opportunity to improve and grow. In summary, I am a graduating Biochemistry senior with a unique blend of scientific knowledge, basic programming experience, and practical computer skills. I am curious, resourceful, and collaborative, and I look forward to using this GitHub portfolio to document my growth and demonstrate my readiness for the next stage of my career.
